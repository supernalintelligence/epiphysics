# Book Outline: The Physics of Common Sense

Companion to [the publishing pathway](./book_publishing_pathway_physics_of_common_sense.md).

This replaces the earlier academic outline. The book is the **everyday edition** by default: plain, relatable, anecdote-first. No philosophy survey, no names you have to look up. It is built directly on the existing **ian.ceo "Physics of Common Sense" blog series**, which already has roughly eleven posts drafted in exactly this voice. That is the head start that makes a one-week build realistic.

## The voice (locked from the existing series)

Every chapter follows the same shape the blog already uses:

1. **Open on a scene you recognize.** "You cleaned your desk on Sunday. By Wednesday it looks like a hurricane passed through."
2. **Say the obvious thing out loud.** You did not plan the mess. It just happened. It happens to everyone.
3. **Reveal the physics already running.** Disorder is free. Order costs energy. That is the second law, and your desk obeys it.
4. **Name one idea, lightly.** Entropy. Free energy. You do not need the equation to feel it.
5. **Land a takeaway you can use Monday.** Maintenance is not a one-time purchase. It is a bill that never stops.

Second person. Short paragraphs. Concrete nouns. The promise from the series intro holds: *you already know physics, you just do not know you know it.* Keep equations optional and in sidebars. The reader should never be blocked by math.

## Build status (why this is fast)

Many chapters already exist as posts in `../nextjs-github-markdown-blog/docs/musings/existence_physics_and_life/physics_of_common_sense/`. Marked below as **[drafted]** (lift and light-edit) or **[to write]** (new, but short, ~1,500 to 2,500 words each, on the same template).

Drafted and ready: messy desk, habits, house cost, best description wins, energy of knowledge, daily entropy, daily physics, time destroyers, thermodynamics of systems, entropy/durability/complexity, potential entropy.

## The book: four parts, anecdote to pattern

The progression mirrors the series: start with what everything is made of, then why things move or stick, then why groups act unlike individuals, then the deeper patterns. Easy things first, each chapter a little less obvious than the last.

### Opening
- **Title page, copyright, epigraph.** Epigraph candidate: a proverb everyone knows, like "a place for everything and everything in its place," then the book quietly explains why that is so hard.
- **Introduction: You already know physics.** Two pages, lifted from the series intro. The momentum you can't get on a project, the inertia of a company that won't change, the way things fall apart if you stop maintaining them. These are not metaphors. They are the same equations in a different state space. Promise: each chapter starts with something you know.

### Closing
- **Conclusion: the bill always comes due.** Pull the thread through: order costs energy, heavy things resist, groups are their own creatures, and the cheapest true description wins. You have been doing physics the whole time. Now you can see the equations under it.
- **A note on what is solid and what is still being tested.** One honest page. The everyday observations are rock solid. The claim that one framework ties them together is a working theory with open questions. Say so plainly. This honesty is the book's signature, not a weakness to hide.

## Optional advanced layer (keep, but secondary)

For readers who want the formal scaffolding, offer it as clearly-marked sidebars, an appendix, or a separate "advanced edition." Do not put it in the main flow. It has two pieces:

1. **The equations.** The blog already carries them in sidebars: momentum p = M·Ẋ, the mass tensor M_ij, free energy F = E − T·S, auto-causal density ρ_ac. Keep them boxed and skippable. Anyone can read the whole book without them.
2. **The scholarship (only if you want it).** The everyday claims line up with a real research record, which can sit in endnotes for the curious: naive-physics work on why our motion intuitions are off, core-knowledge studies on what infants already expect, the AI history of how hard "common sense" is to write down, and the sociology of why common sense fails on complex systems. This is background for footnotes, not material for the main text. The reader never needs a philosopher's name to follow a chapter.

The rule: the main book stays at the altitude of the messy desk. The framework and the citations are there for whoever turns to the back, and invisible to whoever does not.

## Daily occurrences we are missing (candidate additions)

The current four parts cover objects, habits, organizations, and ideas. The conspicuous gap is **people, two at a time and in crowds**, which is the most relatable daily domain of all and, as it happens, the one with the strongest research anchor. These additions are anecdote-first like the rest, each with a real research result behind it so the advanced layer stays honest.

### Proposed new part: Why people pull together and apart

The relationship chapters are missing entirely and they are the most universal. This is likely the part readers quote to friends.

| Chapter | Opens with | The physics / research | Framework concept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Why marriages fall apart (and which ones last)** | A couple bickering over dishes, and a 15-minute tape that predicts divorce | Gottman and Murray's marriage equations: difference equations with "emotional inertia" and steady states predict divorce at ~94%; stable couples hold a 5:1 positive-to-negative ratio | Mass and inertia of belief; stability basins; the negative attractor state; ρ_ac (what loops back, lasts) |
| Why it is so hard to leave | The bad job, the bad relationship, the habit you can't quit | Potential wells and escape energy; sunk cost; hysteresis | Basin depth ΔV; activation energy to climb out |
| Why love fades if you stop feeding it | The spark that dims without anything going wrong | Hedonic adaptation, habituation; maintenance has an ongoing cost | Free energy F = E − T·S; the maintenance bill, applied to feeling |
| Why you become like the people around you | Falling into step, picking up a friend's phrases, a room clapping as one | Coupled oscillators sync above a coupling threshold: Kuramoto, Strogatz, Huygens's two clocks on one beam, audiences clapping in unison | Coupling strength; synchronization as a coupling phenomenon |

This part is the marquee. The divorce chapter alone is worth the price of the book: a famous, replicated result that *is* the framework's language (inertia, steady states, attractors) without anyone having dressed it that way for a general reader.

### Lighter everyday hooks (interludes or a "more physics of your day" appendix)

Short, fun, mostly already sketched in the blog's `daily_physics.md`: why toast lands butter-side down, why toys spread through the house (diffusion), why meetings expand to fill the time (Parkinson's law as a gas filling its container), why your stuff seems to wear out all at once (the bathtub failure curve), why cables and earbuds always tangle (knot probability). Use two or three as palate cleansers between the heavier chapters.

### How this changes the shape

With the additions, the arc grows from four parts to five, and the human one is the emotional center:

1. What everything is made of (desk, knowing, lasting)
2. Why things change or won't (habits, arguments, crises, rumors, getting started)
3. Why groups differ from people (organizations, intentions, crowds, traffic, cities, breaking points, queues)
4. Why the world works this way (house cost, surviving ideas, prediction, rich-get-richer, healing, best description)
5. **Why people pull together and apart** (marriage and divorce, leaving, fading love, becoming like your circle)

Recommended priority for writing: do the **divorce/marriage chapter first** after the drafted ones. It is the most relatable, has the best research, and demonstrates the whole thesis in one famous example.

## What changed from the first outline

The first version led with Reid, Moore, and the philosophy of common sense. That was the wrong altitude: too abstract, names the reader should not have to know, and not how the blog actually sounds. This version leads with the desk, the habit, and the slow company, and demotes all of that scholarship to optional back matter.
